How the moment works

Four steps, every time.

  1. The signalSomething you already record says a person is now waiting, and roughly how long for.
  2. The predictionHow long this one will really take — not the average, this one.
  3. The valueSomething worth their attention goes into that window: an answer, a credit, a thing prepared while they wait.
  4. The exchangeThey get value. You get someone who stayed, and one optional answer you have always wanted.

The money, in plain words

Financial certainty, not fine print.

Summerset's real model, made legible: a one-off licence payment, a deferred management fee of 25% over four years, fees that stop the day she permanently vacates, and a weekly fee whose annual increase will never exceed the rise in the after-tax rate of NZ Superannuation. That last one is published — in the village's statutory disclosure statement on the Retirement Villages Register, not on the website. Which is the whole point. For clarity — never a sale.

$850,000
Summerset's deferred management fee is 25% of the licence payment, accruing over four years in an independent living home (two in a serviced apartment). Their FAQ is explicit about the ending: the weekly fee stops, and the deferred management fee stops accruing, the day she permanently vacates.
    The Summerset Sure 90-day money-back guarantee, in their words: a full refund without any deduction for the deferred management fee if, 90 days after taking up permanent residence, she decides Summerset is not for her (some conditions apply). Nothing here is a commitment.

    The boundary

    What this concept does not do.

    No production access is requested by this concept. No resident record, no care plan and no clinical data is involved or asked for. Everything here is built from what Summerset already publishes, plus what a family types into an enquiry form. Scope, privacy, security and commercial terms would need joint approval before any pilot began.

    01 · the scope

    One region’s information-pack enquiries. Four weeks. Opt-in only.

    02 · the access

    Read-only public content plus what the family types. Draft-write only to the pack. Nothing written to any Summerset system.

    03 · the scorecard

    Enquiry-to-visit rate up, time to a first useful answer down, zero unapproved sends.

    Fail any line of the scorecard and we change the design or stop.

    Before you decide

    What a pilot actually asks of you.

    Written out in full, because the honest version is short and most of it is your time, not your money.

    01

    One named person

    The approver. Every draft stops with them and nothing reaches a customer until they say so. About two hours a week, reading drafts rather than managing a project.

    02

    One situation, not the business

    One village and one decision journey, for six weeks — from the information pack through to the ninety days after moving in. Not the whole portfolio, and nothing to do with care.

    03

    Read-only access, nothing that writes

    Your published information pack, the fee documents and the Summerset Sure terms, and a named person in sales. No resident data, no care records, nothing that writes.

    04

    Your actual rules

    The policies, limits and words the drafts are held against. Every rule shown on this page is a placeholder standing in for yours. An afternoon with whoever owns them is usually enough.

    05

    Six weeks, then a real decision

    Scored on: Drafts an adviser sends without rewriting them. Questions a family got answered without waiting for a callback. Whether the ninety days felt supported rather than watched. And: would that adviser be annoyed if you switched it off? Fail any line of that and we change the design or stop. You keep everything drafted either way.

    06

    A fixed fee, agreed in writing first

    No number is published on this page, because it depends on which situation you pick. Whatever it is, it is agreed before any work starts and it does not move.
