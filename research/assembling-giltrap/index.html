04 — THE WAIT
Four months of nothing, currently

The longest wait in retail, and nobody is using it.

Order a car and you wait months. Right now that wait is an occasional email and a lot of silence — and silence is where cancellations and buyer's remorse live. It is also the single best window you will ever get to learn what this customer actually needs.

So the wait earns them something real, and it prepares their car at the same time. Nothing here is a discount, and nothing here is a survey.

What the customer gets

A credit, not a countdown.

Every stage the car passes through releases a credit toward their ownership — first service, ceramic coating, a towbar, a home charger assessment. Real value, chosen by them, redeemable at the site that sold them the car.

It costs the group far less than the discount it replaces, and unlike a discount it brings them back through the door.

What the group gets

A car that arrives already right.

Each stage asks one optional question. By delivery the site knows whether they tow, whether they ski, who else drives it, whether they can charge at home, and when their current registration expires. Accessories are pre-fitted, the handover is built around them, and the first service is already booked.

✓
One question at a time, all of them optional
No finance data is ever requested. Nothing is passed to a third party. A person confirms every fitting and every booking before it happens.

09 — HOW IT GOES IN
The part most vendors skip

No migration. No new system for anybody to learn.

The reason group marketing tools fail is not capability, it is installation. This one is deliberately designed to be switched on beside what you already run, and to be switched off again without leaving a hole.

01

A scheduled export

Whatever DMS you run already produces stock, sales and service reports. Point one of them at a folder on a schedule. That is the integration.

No API project · no vendor sign-off
02

A folder of brand assets

Group guidelines and each distributor's CI pack, as they already exist. The system reads them as rules and versions them, so the current pack is always the one in use.

Read-only · nothing is edited
03

Two read-only logins

Meta and GA4, read-only. It watches and recommends; it never spends. Media stays exactly where it is today, with the people who run it today.

No spend authority · ever

Everything else — the creative queue, the meeting notes, the events calendar, the campaign ideas — needs nothing from IT at all. If it is not earning its place in six weeks, the export stops and nothing else changes.

10 — COMPLIANCE, IN PRINCIPLE
Before a person ever sees it

A general model will write you a Fair Trading breach and not mention it.

A system drafting across twenty-four marques, on live inventory, into paid media, is exactly where a compliance problem enters unnoticed. Every draft is checked, and each check names the rule it holds.

Under the Fair Trading Act 1986 a company faces penalties of up to $600,000 per breach, and an individual up to $200,000. (Motor Trade Association, dealer legal guidance.)

12 — WHAT WE ARE ACTUALLY ASKING
The boundary

What this will never do

It does not send. It drafts, and a named person sends. It does not publish to any channel, write back to your DMS, submit to a distributor portal, book or buy media, commit co-op funds, move money, or price a vehicle. It never touches customer finance data. It records a meeting only with everyone's consent, and any participant can stop it. It does not pretend to be a person — every draft says it was prepared by a machine and approved by a human.

No production access is requested by this concept.

The pilot ask
1

Scope

Two marques and one dealer principal, for six weeks. Live-inventory campaign ideas, the CI guard, and the meeting loop. Not media buying, not the whole group.

2

Access

One scheduled stock export, your group brand guidelines and two distributor CI packs, and read-only Meta and GA4. No DMS write access. No customer data. No spend authority.

3

Scorecard

Drafts accepted without rewrite. Campaign ideas a DP actually ran. Compliance flags a person agreed with. Time from a DP asking to the work landing. And the one that matters: would that dealer principal be annoyed if you switched it off?
