01 — THE WAIT
A Sunday, 8:15pm

The forty-minute shop

Someone works through the same categories in the same order they did last week, adding the same items, searching for the one thing that moved aisles, and forgetting the thing they will need on Thursday. Nothing about this wait is a surprise, and none of it is being used.

A loyalty programme already knows what a household buys. Using that to award points at the till is the smallest possible use of it. Using it to give someone their Sunday back is the largest.

Live — the wait itself, not a picture of it. It stops to ask you one thing.

What happens now

Forty minutes of manual re-entry against a system that already knows the answer.

What it costs

A routine that feels like a chore is the routine most easily won by whoever makes it shorter.

What the concept does

Drafts the week from what this household actually buys, and stops. Nothing is purchased without review.

03 — THE BOUNDARY

It has read three years of your shopping. Where does it stop?

A supermarket already holds the most revealing dataset in retail. A basket says what you eat, who lives with you, what you can afford and sometimes what you are being treated for. These are the five things a grocery agent gets asked to do, and the line runs through the middle of them.

09 — WHAT WE ARE ACTUALLY ASKING
The boundary

What this will never do

It does not send. It prepares, and a named person sends. It does not publish to any channel, commit spend, move money, or make a decision that belongs to a person. It does not pretend to be a person — every draft says it was prepared by a machine and approved by a human.

No production access is requested by this concept.

The pilot ask
1

Scope

In a real engagement: the weekly draft basket for opted-in households at one store, six weeks. Not pricing, not promotions, not fulfilment, not payment.

2

Access

Range and stock feeds, and the field names for purchase history, stated dietary needs, stated caps and restricted lines. No payment data. No health information.

3

Scorecard

Baskets a customer approved with fewer than three edits. Minutes returned per shop. Held cases a person agreed should have been held. And whether a category manager can find a single line they would call advertising.

Fail any line of the scorecard and we change the design or stop.
