the interval

Six parts assemble around a centre that stays open on purpose. The open centre is the customer, and the named person responsible for what happens next — assembl coordinates the system, and never swallows the person.

assembl designs and runs agentic customer journeys. assembling is the wait-state layer: it turns a natural waiting moment into useful, permissioned, rewarded work that improves the customer's next step — while a named human stays in control.

the six parts

  1. contextwhat is already true — gathered once, carried forward.
  2. permissionwhat the customer agreed to, at the point of use, revocable in one action.
  3. runtimethe bounded machinery that runs the journey.
  4. specialistsnarrow agents with named jobs, visible only at handover.
  5. active waitthe interval itself — a real delay, made useful.
  6. proofthe receipt. It lands last, and the system reaches 100% only when it does.

The seventh part is not a part. The open centre is the customer and the named human decision owner — the one thing The Interval never contains.
