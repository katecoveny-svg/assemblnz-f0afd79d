## How to read the labels

Every claim carries one of three labels.

- **[PUBLISHED]** — the organisation's own material: its website, its statutory disclosure statement, its annual report, its newsroom.
- **[REPORTED]** — press, trade press or analyst. Second-hand.
- **[INFERENCE]** — mine. Not sourced, and marked so you never quote it as fact.

Where I could not source something, it is in *What I could not verify* at the end rather than softened into the body.

## Two corrections to our existing notes

Before anything else, because both were live in our own material.

**1. Ryman's standard deferred management fee is 30%, not 20%.** Ryman's own published article states: "The DMF is calculated as a percentage of the entry price and Ryman's standard DMF is calculated at 30%." **[PUBLISHED]** — [rymanhealthcare.co.nz/what-we-offer/useful-articles/deferred-management-fee](https://www.rymanhealthcare.co.nz/what-we-offer/useful-articles/deferred-management-fee), Ryman Healthcare. The same 30% figure appears on Ryman's Financial Certainty page — [rymanhealthcare.co.nz/financial-certainty](https://www.rymanhealthcare.co.nz/financial-certainty) — and on its Australian site — [rymanhealthcare.com.au/planning-retirement/useful-articles/deferred-management-fee](https://www.rymanhealthcare.com.au/planning-retirement/useful-articles/deferred-management-fee).

The 20% figure was correct historically. RNZ reported on 2 September 2024 that Ryman was moving from 20% to "25 or 30 percent, depending on the initial entry price paid" **[REPORTED]** — [rnz.co.nz/news/business/526811](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/business/526811/ryman-healthcare-raises-prices-to-fill-financial-holes), RNZ. Ryman told The Weekly Source: "There will never be changes to the DMFs or fixed for life weekly fees of our current Ryman residents." **[REPORTED]** — [theweeklysource.com.au](https://www.theweeklysource.com.au/under-pressure-ryman-healthcare-reviewing-its-20-dmf-set-for-life-weekly-fees-and-developments/), The Weekly Source, 6 August 2024.

So there are two populations of Ryman residents on different terms. Any concept that quotes a DMF must ask which cohort the family is in, or it will be wrong for half of them.

**2. Summerset *does* publish a link between its weekly fee and NZ Superannuation.** Our note said it does not. That is true of summerset.co.nz. It is **not** true of Summerset's statutory disclosure statement, which is a public document filed on the Retirement Villages Register. The Summerset on the Landing disclosure statement, disclosure date 1 January 2026, states verbatim:

> "The Weekly Fee will increase annually on 1 July each year. The annual increase will be the same as the percentage increase in the after-tax New Zealand Superannuation Rate, based on the percentage increase for the previous 12-month period, 1 April to 31 March and will be advised to you before 1 June each year."

and, in the summary section:

> "This fee may increase annually, however, any percentage increase in this fee will never exceed the percentage increase in the after tax rate of New Zealand Superannuation in that year."

**[PUBLISHED]** — Disclosure Statement, Summerset on the Landing, disclosure date 1 January 2026, operator Summerset Villages (Kenepuru) Limited, filed on the Retirement Villages Register, New Zealand Companies Office — [srp.companiesoffice.govt.nz](https://srp.companiesoffice.govt.nz/_entity/annotation/ee45c57e-6ddc-f011-8544-002248921145/f3ede290-2de6-413d-89c9-2bf1566d117f?t=1780790400221).

This correction matters more than the fact itself, and it is the single most useful thing I found. **The answers families actually want are published — in 42-page statutory PDFs on a Companies Office register, not on the operator's website.** That gap is the whole opportunity, and it is defensible ground because nothing has to be invented to fill it.

One caution: the disclosure statement is per village and per operator entity. The NZ Super clause is verified for Summerset on the Landing (Kenepuru) as at 1 January 2026. I did not check all 38 villages. Treat it as village-specific until confirmed across the estate.

## NZ regulation and sensitivity

### The statute and the codes

The governing instruments, all **[PUBLISHED]** via Retirement Commission Te Ara Ahunga Ora — [retirement.govt.nz/retirement-villages/the-act-regulations-and-codes](https://retirement.govt.nz/retirement-villages/the-act-regulations-and-codes/):

- Retirement Villages Act 2003
- Retirement Villages (General) Regulations 2006
- Retirement Villages (Dispute Panel) Regulations 2006
- Retirement Villages (Fees) Regulations 2006
- Retirement Villages (General) Amendment Regulations 2014
- Retirement Villages Code of Practice 2008 — amended October 2013 and April 2017
- Code of Residents' Rights — Schedule 4 of the Act

The Code of Practice "sets the minimum standards of how a village must operate" and covers required occupation right agreement terms and operator policies. The General Regulations "prescribe what must be included in key disclosure documents, occupation right agreements, registration requirements and deed of supervision arrangements with statutory supervisors." **[PUBLISHED]** — same source.

### The statutory protections a concept must not tread on

From the Summerset on the Landing disclosure statement **[PUBLISHED]**, which reproduces the Act's requirements:

- **15 working days to cancel.** "Under the Retirement Villages Act 2003, you are entitled to cancel your Occupation Right Agreement within 15 Working Days of signing it."
- **Independent legal advice is mandatory, not optional.** "You must obtain advice from a lawyer independent of the operator of the village before you sign an Occupation Right Agreement."
- **A statutory supervisor holds a first ranking mortgage** in residents' favour, and there is "a memorial on the title to the Land which protects your right to continue living in the Village no matter what happens to Summerset."
- **A four-step disputes path**: the operator's own complaints process, the statutory supervisor, independent mediation, then the disputes panel under the Act.

Any tool that gives a family a number, a comparison or a recommendation sits *upstream* of mandatory independent legal advice. It must hand off to that lawyer rather than substitute for them. This is not a nice-to-have — it is the design constraint that keeps the whole category safe.

### The regulator has already named the hard questions

This is the find I would build the entire pitch on. The Act's prescribed disclosure-statement wording tells intending residents, verbatim:

> "It is common for there to be misunderstandings by residents and their families about:
> - the kind of legal interest that the resident has in the village
> - what happens if the resident or their family wants to exit an Occupation Right Agreement
> - the fees and charges that apply to entering, moving between units within, and leaving the village
> - the ongoing fees and charges."

**[PUBLISHED]** — Disclosure Statement, Summerset on the Landing, 1 January 2026, Companies Office Retirement Villages Register.

The statute names the four things families misunderstand, and explicitly says *"and their families."* An operator that answers those four well is not doing marketing — it is doing what the Act says is commonly done badly. That is the framing for both pitches, and it is unarguable because it is the regulator's own language.

### The reform, and why it changes the brief

The Act is mid-reform. **[PUBLISHED]** — Ministry of Housing and Urban Development, page last updated 4 December 2025 — [hud.govt.nz/our-work/retirement-villages-act-2003](https://www.hud.govt.nz/our-work/retirement-villages-act-2003):

- **Repayments** — "A 12-month maximum repayment timeframe will be introduced", interest payable from six months after move-out, early access available for hardship or special needs, some exemptions for villages under 50 units.
- **Weekly fees and capital losses** — "Weekly fees will stop and fixed deductions will not accrue any higher when a resident moves out" and "Residents will not be liable for capital losses unless they benefit from capital gains."
- **Chattels and fixtures** — operators responsible for items they own and their maintenance; inventory lists required.
- **Disputes** — "A new, independent disputes scheme that's efficient and easy for residents to use will be established", operator-funded.
- **Disclosure and marketing** — simplified disclosure statements and ORAs; "Disclosure rules and obligations will be strengthened"; and the one that bites: **"The Registrar of Retirement villages will have new powers related to advertising material and registered documents that could mislead or deceive people."**

Timeline: an amendment bill "is expected to be introduced in the House by mid-2026" **[PUBLISHED]** — [hud.govt.nz/news/significant-changes-to-the-retirement-village-sector](https://www.hud.govt.nz/news/significant-changes-to-the-retirement-village-sector), announced 4 December 2025. Earlier, the three named review priorities were "Maintenance and repairs of operator-owned chattels and fixtures", "Managing complaints and disputes", and "Options for incentivising or requiring earlier capital repayments when residents move out of a village" **[PUBLISHED]** — Hon Tama Potaka, Associate Minister of Housing, 16 October 2024 — [beehive.govt.nz/release/priorities-set-retirement-villages-act-review](https://www.beehive.govt.nz/release/priorities-set-retirement-villages-act-review).

The Retirement Commissioner, Jane Wrightson, welcomed the package on 4 December 2025, noting reforms follow five years of review and "11,000+ submissions" on the 2023 discussion paper, and that "Operators must publish current disclosure statements online and strengthen obligations to ensure information is not misleading or deceptive." **[PUBLISHED]** — [retirement.govt.nz/news/latest-news/retirement-commission-welcomes-reform-of-retirement-villages-act](https://retirement.govt.nz/news/latest-news/retirement-commission-welcomes-reform-of-retirement-villages-act).

Sector scale, for context: "Over 53,000 people currently reside in retirement villages, with projections of nearly 113,000 by 2048" **[PUBLISHED]** — Beehive release, 16 October 2024, as above.

**Two consequences for every concept in this file.**

First, **an AI that generates a personalised cost estimate is advertising material.** Once the Registrar has powers over "advertising material … that could mislead or deceive", a generated number is not a neutral calculation — it is a representation by the operator. Any concept must therefore compute only from the operator's own registered documents, cite the clause, show the assumptions, and refuse to answer where the document is silent. Generative freehand pricing is off the table.

Second, **"operators must publish current disclosure statements online"** turns the reform into the business case. The obligation is coming anyway. A concept that makes the disclosure statement genuinely legible is compliance work that happens to be excellent marketing, which is a much better funding story than "a new AI channel".

### Privacy — and the part everyone will miss

- **Privacy Act 2020** and the 13 information privacy principles govern all of this. The Office of the Privacy Commissioner published AI guidance in September 2023 (preliminary guidance June 2023), framed around four questions: "Is the training data behind an AI tool relevant, reliable, and ethical?", "What was the purpose for collecting personal information? is your use related?", "How are you testing that AI tools are accurate and fair for your intended purpose?", and "What are you doing to track and manage new risks to information from AI tools?" The guidance cautions: "If in doubt, we recommend you do not use AI tools to handle personal information." **[PUBLISHED]** — [privacy.org.nz/resources-and-learning/a-z-topics/ai](https://www.privacy.org.nz/resources-and-learning/a-z-topics/ai/).
- **IPP3A came into force in May 2026.** It requires an agency collecting personal information about someone *from a source other than that person* to take reasonable steps to make that person aware of the collection, its purpose, the intended recipients, the collecting and holding agencies, and their access and correction rights — "as soon as is reasonably practicable after the information has been collected." **[PUBLISHED]** — [privacy.org.nz/privacy-act-2020/privacy-principles/3a](https://www.privacy.org.nz/privacy-act-2020/privacy-principles/3a/).

  **This is the sharpest constraint in the whole category and it is four months old.** The core scenario for every concept here — an adult child typing their parent's age, health status, mobility and finances into an operator's tool — *is* indirect collection of the parent's personal information. IPP3A means the parent must be told. Every concept below therefore has to answer "what does Mum get told, and when?" A concept that quietly builds a profile of a parent from a child's answers is now not merely distasteful; it is likely non-compliant. Designed well, the notification is the feature — it is what makes the tool trustworthy rather than covert.
- **Codes of practice in force** under the Privacy Act 2020 include the **Health Information Privacy Code 2020** and the **Biometric Processing Privacy Code 2025**; the codes "were updated early in 2026 to incorporate Information Privacy Principle 3A." **[PUBLISHED]** — [privacy.org.nz/privacy-act-2020/codes-of-practice](https://www.privacy.org.nz/privacy-act-2020/codes-of-practice/).

  Consequence: care needs are health information under the HIPC. And any concept touching cameras, voice identification or gait analysis engages the Biometric Processing Privacy Code 2025 — which rules out casually adding fall-detection vision to a family-facing product.

### AI policy settings in New Zealand

New Zealand's Strategy for Artificial Intelligence: Investing with Confidence was published July 2025 by MBIE. It is adoption-focused and does not create an AI-specific regulatory regime; its "Responsible AI guidance for businesses" is described as a voluntary resource. **[PUBLISHED]** — [mbie.govt.nz/…/new-zealands-ai-strategy-investing-with-confidence](https://www.mbie.govt.nz/business-and-employment/economic-growth/digital-policy/new-zealands-ai-strategy-investing-with-confidence); strategy PDF at [beehive.govt.nz](https://www.beehive.govt.nz/sites/default/files/2025-07/New%20Zealand's%20AI%20Strategy%20-%20Investing%20with%20confidence.pdf).

**[INFERENCE]** No AI-specific statute means the binding constraints on these concepts are the *sector* ones: the Retirement Villages Act and the incoming Registrar advertising powers, the Fair Trading Act 1986 on misleading conduct, the Privacy Act with IPP3A and the HIPC, and — care-side — the Ngā Paerewa Health and Disability Services Standard. Pitch the sector regime, not "AI regulation". A CTO in this sector is not worried about an AI Act; they are worried about the Registrar and the auditor.

### Sensitivity: what must not be built

Stated plainly, because several otherwise attractive mechanics fail here.

- **No urgency, scarcity or countdowns.** "3 villas left at this price" against a family deciding about a parent's decline is indefensible, and post-reform it is a Registrar problem as well as an ethical one.
- **No gamification.** No streaks, badges, progress bars or scores on a decision about a parent's health or a family's grief.
- **No inferred health status presented as fact.** A model guessing "your mother is likely to need hospital-level care within 18 months" is clinical prediction dressed as marketing. It engages the HIPC, it may be wrong, and it is cruel if it is right.
- **No profiling a parent behind their back.** IPP3A, and basic decency.
- **No AI-voiced calls to an older person.** Synthetic voice to over-75s who did not ask for it is straightforwardly wrong, whatever the consent position.
- **No lead scoring that changes what a family is told.** Ranking families by likelihood to buy and varying the information given is exactly what the incoming misleading-conduct powers exist to catch.
- **Bereavement is not a campaign trigger.** If a resident dies, every automated sequence stops. Full stop.

### Fees — from the statutory disclosure statement

Source for this whole block: **[PUBLISHED]** Disclosure Statement, Summerset on the Landing, disclosure date 1 January 2026, operator Summerset Villages (Kenepuru) Limited, Retirement Villages Register, NZ Companies Office — [srp.companiesoffice.govt.nz](https://srp.companiesoffice.govt.nz/_entity/annotation/ee45c57e-6ddc-f011-8544-002248921145/f3ede290-2de6-413d-89c9-2bf1566d117f?t=1780790400221).

> "The standard Deferred Management Fee is 25% of the Licence Payment and is accrued over four years for an Independent Living Unit and two years for a Serviced Apartment."

**The trap checks out: 25%, not 30%.** The full accrual mechanics, verbatim:

| Home type | Accrual | Maximum |
|---|---|---|
| Independent Living Unit | "5% of your Licence Payment will accrue … on the Commencement Date. An additional 5% will accrue per annum for a maximum period of four years" | 25% |
| Serviced Apartment | "5% … on the Commencement Date. An additional 10% will accrue per annum for a maximum period of two years" | 25% |
| Care Suite | "5% … on the Commencement Date. An additional 12.5% will accrue in year 1, and 10% will accrue in year 2" | **27.5%** |

All "calculated on a daily basis". A bespoke DMF may be agreed and then overrides the standard.

The DMF "will stop accruing on the earlier of": the due date for payment of the Repayment Sum, expiry of the maximum accrual period, or "the date that you vacate your Home."

Other verified fee facts:

- **Repayment Sum** — "the Licence Payment … less deductions for the Deferred Management Fee …, any outstanding Weekly Fees, Service Package Fees, Daily Care Fees". - **Weekly fee** — "paid monthly in advance and ceases when you vacate your Home"; increases annually on 1 July, capped at the after-tax NZ Super increase (verbatim quoted earlier in this file). Covers, verbatim: maintenance of gardens and grounds; maintenance and repair; cleaning of house exterior and exterior windows; council rates and charges; insurance for the Village and the fixtures and fittings in your Home; 24 hour alarm call system monitoring and response; regular activities and entertainment; weekly happy hour; regular Village outings; overall management of the Village.
- **Transfer administration fee** — "A transfer administration fee of 2% of the licence payment for the new home will apply." Waived for within-village moves from an Independent Living Unit to a Serviced Apartment or Care Suite, or Serviced Apartment to Care Suite.
- **No additional upfront payment on a health-driven move** — "If your health needs necessitate a transfer into a Serviced Apartment or Care Suite we will not require you to make any additional upfront payment in order to make the transfer."
- **DMF capped across moves** — "your Deferred Management Fee will not exceed 25% overall (where the standard Deferred Management Fee applies)", with accrued percentage carried across.
- **Dual Occupancy Fee** — where one of a couple moves to a Care Suite and the other stays: "currently capped at 10% of the Licence Payment for the Care Suite, with 5% accruing on the Commencement Date and a further 5% accruing on day 366". ### Summerset Sure — verified, and more nuanced than we had it

The guarantee is real, but it is **tiered by home type**, and our notes had only the 90-day tier.

From the FAQ **[PUBLISHED]** — [summerset.co.nz/living-at-summerset/frequently-asked-questions](https://www.summerset.co.nz/living-at-summerset/frequently-asked-questions/):

> "Our Summerset Sure 90-day money-back guarantee means that you will get a full refund without any deduction for the deferred management fee if, on the expiry of 90 days after taking up permanent residence in your independent living home or serviced apartment, you decide that Summerset is not for you (some conditions apply)."

> "The only costs you may have to pay are any costs you have incurred for legal advice, together with the weekly fee and any other outstanding charges. The Summerset Sure 90-day money back guarantee does not apply if you pass away or leave for health reasons."

From *Understanding the cost for care* **[PUBLISHED]**, S0776/NOV 21 — the tier our notes missed:

> "30 days to decide if your occupation right for your memory care apartment or premium room is right for you; or 90 days to decide if your serviced apartment is right for you."

And the disclosure statement adds a third exclusion — transfers:

> "In addition to this, Summerset offers a 'love it or your money back' promise. … The 'love it or your money back' promise does not apply if you have transferred to your Home from another Summerset home, if you leave your Home due to your health needs, or in the event of death."

So: **90 days** for an independent living home or serviced apartment, **30 days** for a memory care apartment or premium room, and it does **not** apply on transfer, on health-driven exit, or on death. Note the exclusions are precisely the exits a family in a care transition is most likely to face — worth handling honestly rather than leading with the guarantee.

- Promise: the guides "introduce you to life at Ryman, showcase our independent and assisted living options, and give clarity around the costs."
- Delivery: **emailed, not instant.** "After submitting the form, you'll receive an email with links to the guides."

**[INFERENCE]** Ryman promises "clarity around the costs" and then makes the visitor wait for an email — while its own site does not publish the DMF accrual period. The gap between the promise and the wait is Ryman's specific opportunity, and it is a different gap from Summerset's.

### Technology

**myRyman Care is real but is not currently marketed to families.** Neither the homepage, the investors page, nor the useful-articles hub mentions myRyman, technology, apps or AI. **[PUBLISHED]** — pages as cited above.

Our earlier notes hold detail on myRyman Care — Rick Davies as Chief Technology and Innovation Officer, roughly 3,500 tablets, a $20M+ programme, the 2019 Asia Pacific Eldercare Innovation Award — sourced previously to rymanhealthcare.co.nz, careers.rymanhealthcare.com, healthcareitnews.com and news.microsoft.com/apac. I did **not** re-verify those figures in this pass and they are listed in *What I could not verify*. Treat myRyman as: real, clinical, staff-facing, and invisible to the buying family.

**[INFERENCE]** This is the strategic read for Ryman. They have already proved they build and ship their own clinical technology. Any assembl concept must be complementary and family-facing, never a replacement for myRyman Care, or it will be heard as an attack on the CTIO's own programme.

## Four concepts for Summerset

Summerset's specific gap, in one line: **every number is deferred to a couriered pack, while every number is already public in a statutory PDF nobody reads.** All four concepts work that seam. Brand line to stay inside: "Love the life you choose" — the concepts should widen choice, never narrow it.

A rule that applies to all four: **they compute only from Summerset's own registered documents and quote the clause.** No generated pricing, no estimates, no "typically". Once the Registrar has powers over misleading advertising material, that is the only safe architecture — and it is also the only honest one.

### 1. While the Pack's in the Post

**Waiting moment** — the days between requesting the free information pack and the courier arriving. Summerset's own site sends every number into that pack, so the wait is currently dead air.

**What the family sees** — one page, sent to whoever requested the pack, for the specific village they asked about. It answers the four things the Act says residents and their families commonly misunderstand: the legal interest they'd hold, what happens on exit, the fees for entering, moving within, and leaving, and the ongoing fees. Every figure is drawn from that village's registered disclosure statement with the clause quoted and linked. Where the document is silent it says so and offers a human.

**Hard question answered** — "what does it really cost, and what do we get back?"

**Funded by** — sales and marketing, on pack-to-visit conversion. Co-owned with legal and compliance, because the incoming obligation to "publish current disclosure statements online" makes this a compliance asset that happens to be good marketing.

**Measured on** — share of pack recipients who arrive at a first visit already understanding the DMF and repayment mechanics; fall in late-stage "I didn't realise" objections logged by sales; and **families who self-select out early**, counted as a success, not a leak.

**Nearest real precedent** — Roobrik Surveys and Roobrik Afford: decision support placed deliberately before sales contact, for visitors "not ready for sales contact". Named customers Brightview Senior Living, Arete Living, Cedar Community; published claims "20-40% increase in quality web leads" and "up to 2x better SQL to Move-in rate vs other web leads". **[PUBLISHED]** — [roobrik.com](https://www.roobrik.com/), [roobrik.com/afford-senior-living-survey](https://roobrik.com/afford-senior-living-survey/). Note Roobrik's design choice worth copying: "no numbers required".

### 2. Same Page

**Waiting moment** — the weeks between a first village visit and a family decision, when siblings in three cities are forwarding each other PDFs and half-remembered phone calls.

**What the family sees** — one private space per enquiry. Each sibling asks in their own words; everyone gets the same answer, sourced to the same clause. A standing "what we don't know yet" list. A record of who has seen what, so no one can be accused of deciding without the others.

**Hard question answered** — "are we deciding this together, and are we all working from the same facts?" This is the sibling problem, and nothing in the NZ market addresses it.

**Funded by** — sales. Clearest commercial case of the four: stalled multi-decision-maker enquiries are the expensive ones.

**Measured on** — share of enquiries with more than one engaged family member; stall rate; volume of repeat explanations by sales staff. Explicitly **not** measured on time-to-close, so the tool is never tuned to hurry a family.

**Nearest real precedent** — the employer-funded eldercare navigation category (Cariloop, Homethrive, Wellthy) and consumer family-coordination tools; see *The family's journey* for what is actually shipped and by whom.

**Where this would be inappropriate** — never score or rank family members; never surface one sibling's private notes to another; never nudge toward a decision or flag "this family is cooling". The moment it recommends, it stops being a shared table and becomes a sales instrument.

### 3. When Things Change

**Waiting moment** — after a needs assessment, before a transfer. The most frightening and most opaque wait in the journey, and it happens to existing residents, so it is invisible to marketing.

**What the family sees** — plainly, what a move from villa to serviced apartment to care suite means at *their* village: DMF already accrued and how it carries across, the 25% overall cap, the 2% transfer administration fee and exactly when it is waived, the Dual Occupancy Fee if one of a couple moves, the daily care fee, and the Residential Care Subsidy and WINZ means-assessment path. Including the reassuring part Summerset already publishes but buries: "If your health needs necessitate a transfer into a Serviced Apartment or Care Suite we will not require you to make any additional upfront payment in order to make the transfer."

**Hard question answered** — "what happens when Mum's care needs change?"

**Funded by** — care operations, not sales. It serves people who have already bought. That is the honest funder and a stronger internal story than a lead metric.

**Measured on** — complaints and disputes about transfer costs; time from needs assessment to a settled family decision; family-reported clarity at the transfer conversation.

**Nearest real precedent** — Summerset's own *Understanding the cost for care* brochure (doc ref S0776/NOV 21), which already does this on paper for care rooms, including a published worked example. This concept is that brochure made live and village-specific. **[PUBLISHED]** — Summerset.

**Where this would be inappropriate** — **it must never predict decline.** It answers "if this happens, here is what it means" and never "your mother will likely need hospital-level care." It is triggered only by a needs assessment the family already holds, never by inference from behaviour, sensor data or staff notes. Predicting a parent's deterioration to their child is clinical prediction wearing a marketing badge, it engages the Health Information Privacy Code 2020, and it is cruel whether or not it is right.

### 4. Read It With Me

**Waiting moment** — the 15 working day statutory cancellation window, and the days waiting on a lawyer's appointment.

**What the family sees** — the occupation right agreement and disclosure statement, readable side by side, with any question answered against the clause. Its actual output is **a numbered list of questions to take to the independent lawyer**. It states on every screen that it is not legal advice and that independent legal advice before signing is mandatory under the Act.

**Hard question answered** — "what am I actually signing, and what should I be asking?"

**Funded by** — legal and compliance with sales. The strongest compliance narrative of the four: it improves the mandatory independent-advice step instead of competing with it.

**Measured on** — number and quality of questions families bring to their lawyer; solicitor feedback on the conversation; and cancellations inside the 15-day window — which may **rise**, and that must be accepted in advance as the tool working correctly.

**Nearest real precedent** — see *The family's journey*; the closest shipped analogues are document-comprehension assistants rather than anything senior-living-specific, which is itself the finding.

**Where this would be inappropriate** — it must refuse to give legal advice, must never call a clause "standard" or "nothing to worry about", and must never imply a lawyer is optional. If it cannot reliably refuse, it should not ship.

## Four concepts for Ryman

Ryman's gap is a different shape. They publish a promise — the guides "give clarity around the costs" — then email a PDF, and their own site does not publish the DMF accrual period. They have two resident cohorts on different fee terms. And they already build real clinical technology in-house, so anything family-facing must be complementary to myRyman Care, never a substitute. Brand line to stay inside: "It's all taken care of" — which means **the operator does the work, not the family.** Ryman's concepts should remove effort; Summerset's should remove opacity.

### 1. The Guide That Answers Back

**Waiting moment** — between submitting the form and the guides email arriving, and then the far longer wait after reading them, when the real questions start.

**What the family sees** — the guides arrive as they do now, plus a private link to ask questions about them. Answers are grounded in Ryman's published material and the specific village's registered disclosure statement. Where Ryman does not publish something — the DMF accrual period being the obvious one — it says exactly that and routes the question to a person, rather than guessing.

**Hard question answered** — "you promised clarity on costs: what is the 30% actually, over what period, and what comes back to us?"

**Funded by** — marketing, on guide-to-visit conversion.

**Measured on** — guide-to-visit rate; and the running list of "we don't publish this" questions, which becomes a prioritised backlog of what Ryman should publish. That second metric is the real product, and it is the one to show a CTO.

**Nearest real precedent** — Roobrik Chat, which pairs customer service with optional decision-support modules on senior living sites. **[PUBLISHED]** — [roobrik.com](https://www.roobrik.com/).

**Where this would be inappropriate** — the "we don't publish this" answer must stay honest. The instant it fills a gap with a plausible-sounding number, it becomes misleading advertising material under the incoming Registrar powers.

### 2. Which Terms Are Ours

**Waiting moment** — the anxious days after a resident or their family reads a news story about Ryman raising fees. This wait is created by press coverage, not by a funnel, which is why nobody owns it.

**What the family sees** — after identity confirmation, the terms that apply to *their* agreement in plain language: DMF percentage, whether their weekly fee is fixed or indexed, the repayment mechanics, and the interest entitlement after six months — each cited to their own document. Plus an unambiguous statement of what has not changed for them.

**Hard question answered** — "has our deal changed, and can they change it later?"

**Funded by** — resident relations and operations, with the CFO's interest, because it defuses a recurring complaints and reputation load.

**Measured on** — fee-related complaint and call volume in the fortnight after fee coverage; call handling time; resident-reported trust.

**Nearest real precedent** — none in senior living that I could source; the closest working analogue is "your policy as it stands today" self-service in banking and insurance. Flagged in *What I could not verify* rather than dressed up.

**Where this would be inappropriate** — no upsell, ever. And access by an adult child requires the resident's consent: IPP3A and the Health Information Privacy Code 2020 both apply. If the resident lacks capacity, it runs through the enduring power of attorney and is documented, never on assumed consent.

### 3. How Mum's Week Was

**Waiting moment** — the long silence between visits, when an adult child in another city has no idea how their parent actually is. Weeks long, repeated indefinitely, and currently filled by phone calls to a village receptionist.

**What the family sees** — with the resident's explicit, revocable consent, a short weekly note in plain language about non-clinical life: what they joined in, how meals went, who visited, how they have seemed. Written from records staff already keep. Never vital signs, never assessments, never a risk score. myRyman Care stays exactly where it is — clinical and staff-facing.

**Hard question answered** — "will Mum actually be happy there?" — asked before the decision, and "is she alright?" — asked forever afterwards.

**Funded by** — operations. It displaces a genuinely large volume of unscheduled calls to village staff, which is a measurable and honest saving rather than a marketing claim.

**Measured on** — unsolicited family calls to village reception; family-reported reassurance; and consent and opt-out rates, where **a high opt-out rate is treated as a design failure**, not as friction to be engineered away.

**Nearest real precedent** — the resident-and-family communication category (Amazon Alexa Smart Properties for Senior Living, K4Connect, Cubigo, Speak2 Family); see *What is actually shipping worldwide* for named deployments.

**Where this would be inappropriate** — **this is the concept most likely to be built badly, and the one to walk away from if the conditions cannot be met.** Non-negotiables: the resident consents and can revoke instantly and alone; the resident sees exactly what their family sees, always; no clinical content; no inference about mood, loneliness or decline; no cameras, no gait analysis, no voice identification — all of which would engage the Biometric Processing Privacy Code 2025. It is a reassurance product or it is nothing. If it starts describing what a parent did rather than what was offered to them, it has become surveillance of an older person for the comfort of a younger one, and it should be stopped.

### 4. Before You Say Anything

**Waiting moment** — the months before any enquiry exists, while an adult child works up to raising the subject. The longest wait in the category and the only one no operator serves.

**What the family sees** — no form, no village, no contact required. A private resource that helps someone prepare for a conversation with a parent who does not want to move, grounded in independent published guidance rather than Ryman's sales material. It does not recommend Ryman. Its whole job is to be the most useful thing available on the day someone searches that question at 11pm.

**Hard question answered** — "how do I raise this with Mum, who does not want to move?" The hardest question in the set, and in New Zealand it is close to unserved: Age Concern NZ publishes on housing options — "Other home options vs staying at home", "How to access home support services", "Staying in your home", "Advanced care planning: Your choice matters" — but nothing addressed to family members on raising relocation with a reluctant parent. **[PUBLISHED]** — [ageconcern.org.nz/information-advice/home-and-housing](https://ageconcern.org.nz/information-advice/home-and-housing/).

**Funded by** — brand, as brand investment, on a multi-year horizon. Be blunt with the CMO: weakest attribution of all eight, strongest brand return, and it only works if nobody attaches a lead target to it.

**Measured on** — brand consideration and assisted recall among 45–65s; organic share of voice on those queries. Deliberately **not** measured on lead capture.

**Nearest real precedent** — the family-conversation guidance published by ageing and dementia charities; see *The questions nobody answers well* for what exists and how good it is.

**Where this would be inappropriate** — this is where pressure would do the most harm and be most tempting. No lead capture, no retargeting pixel, no "families like yours chose Ryman", no follow-up email. **If Ryman cannot accept zero attribution, this concept should not be built** — a version of it with a lead form attached is worse than not doing it, because it monetises the moment a family is most frightened.
