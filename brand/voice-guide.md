# assembl voice guide

_Status_: locked — Reo 2026-05-03

## The assembl voice

1. **Humanistic first.** Every sentence acknowledges the people doing the work. Agents work alongside, never in place of. The human keeps the expertise, the sign-off, and the Friday afternoon.

2. **NZ English.** Colour, not color. Organisation, not organization. Programme, not program. Macrons on all te reo Māori — Māori, tikanga, kaitiakitanga, whānau, Tāmaki Makaurau.

3. **Te reo lives in structure, not on the surface.** Kete names, agent names, pipeline stages, colour tokens — these are te reo. Headlines, taglines, CTAs, email subjects, newsletter mastheads — these are English. The test: would a non-Māori-speaking NZ builder/broker/council officer have to translate before engaging? If yes, move the te reo inward.

4. **Editorial, not marketing.** Write like Wired, The Economist, or the FT — not like a SaaS landing page. No exclamation marks. No hype. No "revolutionise your workflow." State what it does, cite what it checks, show the Evidence Pack.

5. **Draft-only posture in every claim.** assembl drafts. You approve. assembl checks. You decide. assembl documents. You sign. Never imply the agent acts autonomously.

6. **Cite, don't claim.** "Cites Building Act 2004, s 14B" is stronger than "ensures compliance." Name the Act, the Section, the Year. If you can't cite it, don't claim it.

7. **No forbidden language.** Never use: replaces, automate away, eliminates the need for, 10× faster, in seconds, instant, supercharge, revolutionise, unleash, disrupt, game-changing, world-class, cutting-edge, enterprise-grade, synergy, leverage (verb).

8. **The one-line test.** Read your copy aloud. If it would make a foreman, a draughtsperson, a customs broker, or a chef nervous about their job — rewrite it.
