# Costing and prices — every product, kit and lab item (21 October 2026)

**Status: a model, not a quote.** Nothing has been printed, so every print time is estimated from mass, and no partner has quoted. The first print and the first partner quote replace these numbers; the model is `tools/living-cad/launch/cost_model.py`, its table `cost-model.csv`, its inputs `widget-items.json` (from `dump_items.ts`) and the manifest.

## Two cost bases

- **Own print**: a desktop printer at home or in the studio. Filament by mass and material, print time from mass at a deposition rate per material, machine time, handling labour per print job, a failure allowance, packaging, and packing labour once per order.
- **Bureau**: a NZ print service at the rate card in `print_order.py` (NZD per gram by material plus a per-file charge), with the same packing and packaging on top. This is the rough card used for the first-print estimate; a real quote (3DPrintIt, a Treatstock Auckland vendor) replaces it.

Two cost figures per item: **alone** (the item is the whole order, so it carries the full packing and packaging) and **in a basket** (packing and packaging shared across an average order of three items). Prices include 15 % GST. Shipping is not in the unit cost; it is charged to the customer or covered by a free-shipping threshold.

## What the model says

- **Small accessories are under-priced.** Of 40 products, 13 lose money sold alone at the current proposed price (hook, node-corner, node-tee, foot, keys-6, duo-strap, carabiner-loop, home-dock, handline-winder, bag-charm, zip-pull, cable-winder, spoke-beads), and 18 sit under 35 % margin even in a basket. The cause is not plastic: a hook is 14 g (about 70 c of filament) but carries a print job, handling and a share of packing. Below about NZ$12 an item cannot cover its own order; it needs a basket, a minimum order or a free-shipping threshold.
- **Boards, pods, plates and the shade are priced about right** at 45–60 % in a basket; the renter board and the shade are above target and could come down.
- **Living room kits are above target** (56–63 % at the current prices); the model would allow NZ$10–40 less. **dayrally kits are below target** (31–45 %): the ride kit, the desk kit and the scoot kit most of all, because they hold many small parts. The suggested kit prices lift dayrally kits NZ$20–40.
- **The bureau basis is 2–3× the own-print cost** for the same items (per-gram card plus NZ$4 a file), so printing in-house or with a batch quote is the margin. At the suggested prices, the bureau margin is thin to negative on small parts and only around 30 % on kits.
- **Lab items get a first price** here for planning only; they stay unlisted until a test print passes (ADR-010).

## What to do with it

1. Keep the catalogue's proposed prices as they are until the first print measures real print times and a partner quotes; then re-run the model with measured hours and the quote in place of the rate card.
2. Decide the small-item policy: a NZ$12 floor on singles, or a free-shipping threshold around NZ$60 that pulls small parts into baskets. The model's basket cost assumes three items an order.
3. Lift the dayrally kit prices towards the suggested column when they list; they are the kits most exposed.
4. For the girl range and the stationery pieces, price from this model on day one (brief §11.22), so nothing new launches under its own cost.
