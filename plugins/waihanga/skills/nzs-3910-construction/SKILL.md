# NZS 3910 — standard conditions of contract — drafting skill (Waihanga)

## When to use

- Drafting a variation request, variation pricing, or variation
  acceptance under the contract for the engineer-to-the-contract or
  named contract administrator to review and direct.
- Drafting an extension of time (EOT) claim with the cause, the
  affected critical-path activities, and the supporting evidence.
- Drafting practical completion correspondence and the practical
  completion list.
- Drafting defects notification correspondence inside the defects
  notification period (DNP), including the defects schedule.
- Drafting a notice under the dispute resolution sequence (engineer's
  decision → mediation → arbitration or expert determination per the
  contract).

## What this skill will NOT do

- Bind the parties to amendments of the NZS 3910 form. Amendments are
  drafted into Special Conditions and signed by the parties; the
  skill does not sign anything.
- Issue formal directions under the contract on behalf of the
  engineer-to-the-contract or contract administrator. Directions are
  the role-holder's authority, not the drafting tool's.
- Determine the merits of a variation claim, an EOT claim, or a
  defects dispute. The skill surfaces the contract clauses and the
  documentation pattern; the engineer-to-the-contract or named
  decision-maker decides.
- Replace the role of an engineer-to-the-contract, contract
  administrator, or quantity surveyor. The skill drafts; the role
  holders take responsibility.
- Provide legal, engineering, or quantity-surveying advice. Outputs
  are draft work product only.

## Tikanga check

NZS 3910 is a commercial standard form; the contract clauses are
neutral. Where the project is on whenua of cultural significance, the
client is a Māori-owned entity, or the project sits under a Treaty
settlement framework, defer to the tikanga-compliance skill for
relationship and communication norms. Variation, EOT, and defect
correspondence is firm and accurate; manaakitanga applies in tone.

Macrons preserved (Waihanga).

## Privacy Act check

Contract correspondence carries party identities, role-holder names,
and sometimes financial information. These are personal information
for individuals named in the role. Apply IPPs 1, 5, 9, and 11. Project
contract files have a retention period set by the contract and by
professional-indemnity requirements; default to 12 years from practical
completion unless the contract sets a different period.

## Workflow steps

### Variations

1. Identify the trigger — instruction, scope change, or claim of
   constructive change.
2. Confirm the variation procedure required by the contract — written
   instruction, pricing in advance where practicable, agreement on
   time impact.
3. Draft the variation order, the contractor's pricing build-up, and
   the time-impact analysis.
4. Stage for the engineer-to-the-contract or contract administrator
   to review and direct.

### Extensions of time

1. Identify the qualifying cause and the contract clause that allows
   relief.
2. Map the cause onto the critical-path activities, with programme
   evidence (baseline programme, current programme, impact analysis).
3. Draft the EOT notice within the contractual notice period — late
   notices may bar the claim.
4. Stage for the engineer-to-the-contract to review and decide.

### Practical completion

1. Draft the contractor's notice of practical completion.
2. Draft the practical completion list (defects, incomplete items
   acceptable for occupation).
3. Draft the engineer's certificate of practical completion for the
   engineer-to-the-contract to review and issue.
4. Trigger the defects notification period clock from the certified
   date.

### Defects liability

1. Inside the DNP, draft defects notifications as defects are
   identified — defect description, location, contractually
   responsible party, remediation expected.
2. Draft the defects schedule at the end of the DNP.
3. Draft the engineer's certificate of final completion / making good
   for the engineer-to-the-contract to review and issue.

### Dispute resolution

1. Follow the contract's dispute sequence in order — engineer's
   decision is usually first.
2. Draft the notice of dispute, the supporting position paper, and
   the proposed forum (mediation, expert determination, arbitration
   per the contract).
3. Stage for legal review before any notice is issued.
