# NZS 3910 — Waihanga utility skill

## When to use

- Drafting or reviewing a head construction contract under NZS 3910.
- Drafting variations, extensions of time, or payment-certificate
  correspondence under the standard conditions.
- Briefing the Engineer to the Contract on a specific clause.
- Comparing a draft set of special conditions against the published
  standard.
- Producing a plain-English explainer of an NZS 3910 mechanism for a
  non-construction stakeholder.

## What this skill will NOT do

- Bind either party to amendments or special conditions. Drafting
  changes goes to the parties' lawyers; signing binds.
- Issue formal directions under the contract. Only the Engineer to
  the Contract can issue a formal direction within their authority;
  the skill drafts; the Engineer issues.
- Issue payment certificates. The Engineer certifies; the skill helps
  prepare the underlying calculation.
- Replace legal review of special conditions. NZS 3910 is heavily
  amended in practice, and the amendments matter — every special
  conditions schedule is read by a lawyer before signing.
- Resolve a dispute. The skill helps draft notices and prepare
  evidence; mediation, arbitration, or adjudication is conducted by
  the parties and their advisers.

## Tikanga check

Where the project involves a Māori party, marae, papakāinga, kura
kaupapa Māori, or whenua Māori, defer to the building-act-2004 skill's
tikanga note for the wider workflow tone. Whanaungatanga is
particularly relevant in long-running construction relationships —
the same head contractor and subcontractor may work together across
many projects, and contract correspondence carries the tone of that
ongoing relationship.

## Privacy Act check

Contract documents are commercial; personal information appears in
the named-personnel schedules (Engineer, Engineer's Representative,
key personnel) and in any health-and-safety incident records. Apply
IPP 1 minimisation when including personal contact details in the
contract; apply IPP 11 when distributing incident reports.

## Workflow steps

### What NZS 3910 is

NZS 3910 (currently NZS 3910:2023) is the standard New Zealand
conditions of contract for building and civil engineering
construction. It is the most widely used head construction contract
in New Zealand. The Standard is published by Standards New Zealand
and acquired under licence — the skill does not reproduce the
clause text, only references it.

Related standards:

- **NZS 3915** — conditions of contract for subcontracts.
- **NZS 3916** — conditions of contract for design and construction.
- **NZS 3917** — conditions of contract for consultancy services.

### Drafting pattern — special conditions

1. Start with the published standard conditions.
2. Identify which standard clauses the project requires to amend.
3. Draft the amendment as a special condition referencing the
   clause it modifies.
4. Send the special-conditions schedule for legal review **before**
   either party signs. Special conditions can substantially shift
   risk; they are not a templating exercise.

### Drafting pattern — variation

1. Identify the contract change (scope, programme, payment).
2. Draft a variation request from the party initiating the change,
   or a variation instruction from the Engineer if the Engineer is
   directing it.
3. State the price and time effect, with substantiation.
4. Stage for the Engineer to issue (or the parties to agree). The
   skill never issues the variation.

### Drafting pattern — payment certificate support

1. Pull the contractor's progress claim.
2. Reconcile against the schedule of prices, retentions, variations,
   and any liquidated damages.
3. Produce the underlying calculation pack for the Engineer.
4. The Engineer certifies. The skill never certifies.

### Drafting pattern — extension of time

1. Identify the cause of delay and the contract clause under which
   the extension is claimed.
2. Compile the float, programme impact, and contemporaneous records.
3. Stage the EOT submission for the contractor to issue and the
   Engineer to determine.

### Dispute pathway sketch

NZS 3910 disputes typically proceed by negotiation, then mediation,
then arbitration. The Construction Contracts Act 2002 statutory
adjudication right is available regardless of the contract pathway
and runs in parallel — see the construction-contracts-act skill.
