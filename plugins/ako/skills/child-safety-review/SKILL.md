# Child Safety Review — quick reference skill (Ako)

[Kaihanga: scaffold. Draft full body before pilot — this is the
highest-stakes skill in the kete; HITL must be uncompromising.]

## When to use

[Kaihanga: write the trigger conditions — onboarding a new staff
member through the safety-check workflow, drafting an incident
record after a near-miss / injury / behavioural event, refreshing
the centre's child-protection policy, drafting a hazard or risk
register entry, drafting a report-of-concern narrative for the
licensed person responsible.]

## What this skill will NOT do

- Will NOT submit a Police vetting request through the NZ Police
  Vetting Service on a worker's behalf, nor receive or interpret
  a vetting result. Submission and interpretation remain with the
  centre's safety check officer (a defined role under the
  Children's Act 2014).
- Will NOT make a "report of concern" to Oranga Tamariki on the
  centre's or worker's behalf. Reports of concern are made by a
  named professional (s.15 Oranga Tamariki Act 1989) — the agent
  drafts only.
- Will NOT make a binding determination on whether a worker is
  "safe to work with children" under the Children's Act 2014.
  That judgement is the safety check officer's, informed by the
  vetting result, the identity check, the work-history check,
  and the referee check.
- Will NOT respond on the centre's behalf to an Oranga Tamariki
  enquiry, an ERO concern, or an ECE-licensing officer's finding.
  Drafts go to the licensed person responsible and the centre's
  professional adviser.
- Will NOT log a notifiable event to WorkSafe under HSWA 2015.
  Notification remains with the PCBU's H&S-competent person.

## Tikanga check

[Kaihanga: write the tikanga frame — child-safety work touches
whānau, hapū, and iwi connections of tamariki. Reports of concern
involving Māori tamariki carry whakapapa weight; tikanga-compliance
is required on every draft. Manaakitanga and aroha frame the tone
of communication with whānau even in difficult moments. Defers to
tangata whenua / iwi authorities where appropriate.]

## Privacy Act check

[Kaihanga: write the IPP coverage — child-safety records contain
the most sensitive personal information in the centre (minors,
allegations, vetting results, health information). IPP 1
(collection limited to what the Children's Act and Regulations
require), IPP 5 (locked-down access; safety check officer only),
IPP 9 (retention per Children's Act / records-management
practice), IPP 11 (disclosure to Police Vetting, Oranga Tamariki,
ERO — only on a lawful basis), IPP 3A (1 May 2026) for indirectly
collected referee / employer information.]

## Workflow steps

[Kaihanga: write the step-by-step workflow — receive the safety
input (new worker onboarding / incident / near-miss / concern),
classify against Children's Act and Regulations, draft the
record / policy / report-of-concern narrative, run the tikanga
and privacy passes, stage for the safety check officer or the
licensed person responsible — no exceptions, no auto-sends.]
