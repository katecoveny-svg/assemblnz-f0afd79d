# Consumer Law Check — quick reference skill (Hoko)

## When to use

[Kaihanga: write the trigger conditions — triaging an inbound
customer complaint against CGA remedies, drafting a repair /
replacement / refund offer, drafting a Disputes Tribunal response,
drafting a product-recall communication to customers, drafting a
supplier-side remedy claim under CGA s.41.]

## What this skill will NOT do

- Will NOT lodge a Disputes Tribunal claim or response on the
  operator's behalf. Lodgement remains with the named human (the
  operator, the customer, or the operator's solicitor).
- Will NOT make a binding determination on whether a good is of
  "acceptable quality" under CGA s.7, "fit for purpose" under
  s.8, or whether a Fair Trading Act s.9 misleading-conduct
  threshold has been crossed. Refer to the operator's solicitor
  or the marketing-law adviser.
- Will NOT respond on the operator's behalf to a Commerce
  Commission Fair Trading Act enquiry, an MBIE product-recall
  request, or a Disputes Tribunal hearing direction. Drafts go
  to the operator and the operator's solicitor.
- Will NOT issue a recall notice, post a product-safety notice
  on the operator's website, or notify MBIE on the operator's
  behalf. All publication and notification stays with the
  operator's named human.
- Will NOT release a customer's personal information to a
  manufacturer, a supplier, or a regulator without the operator's
  explicit Privacy Act 2020 / IPP 11 sign-off.

## Tikanga check

[Kaihanga: write the tikanga frame — for Māori-owned retailers,

## Privacy Act check

[Kaihanga: write the IPP coverage — customer-complaint records
contain personal information (name, address, purchase history,
sometimes health information for safety incidents). IPP 1
(collection limited to remedy assessment), IPP 5, IPP 9 (retention
per tax and CGA records-management practice), IPP 11 (disclosure
to manufacturer / supplier / Disputes Tribunal — only on a lawful
basis), IPP 3A (1 May 2026) for indirectly collected complainant
information.]
