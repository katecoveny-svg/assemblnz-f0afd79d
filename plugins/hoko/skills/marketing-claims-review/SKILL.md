## What this skill will NOT do

- Will NOT make a binding determination on whether a claim is
  "misleading" under Fair Trading Act s.9 or "false" under s.13.
  Refer to the operator's marketing-law adviser.
- Will NOT release a campaign claim, post a social ad, or update
  product detail pages on the operator's behalf. Publishing
  remains with the operator's brand or campaign manager.
- Will NOT lodge an ASA pre-vet, an ASA complaint, or a Commerce
  Commission complaint on the operator's behalf. Lodgement
  remains with the named human.
- Will NOT respond on the operator's behalf to an ASA complaint
  decision, a Commerce Commission warning letter, or a Fair
  Trading Act prosecution. Drafts go to the marketing-law
  adviser.
- Will NOT use the bare token "AI" in any customer-facing claim
  (canon hard rule #4). Uses "intelligent automation" or describes
  the function — and reviews any third-party copy that uses "AI"
  as a marketing claim against FTA s.9.

## Tikanga check

[Kaihanga: write the tikanga frame — claims using te reo Māori,
referencing taonga ingredients (mānuka, kawakawa, harakeke,
pounamu), or invoking iwi / hapū association require
tikanga-compliance review. Reserved taonga terms not used as
product names. "Made with native ingredients" claims tested
against the actual supply relationship (iwi consent, harvest
permits, sustainable use).]

## Privacy Act check

[Kaihanga: write the IPP coverage — campaign claims that include
customer testimonials or influencer endorsements collect personal
information. IPP 1 (collection limited to consented use), IPP 8
(accuracy — every fact in the claim verifiable), IPP 11
(disclosure of testimonials only with consent), IPP 3A (1 May 2026)
for indirectly collected influencer / partner information.]
