# Auaha — domain stub skill

**STATUS**: scaffold — full skill body deferred until pilot customer
assigned to this kete.

## When to use

This is the broad-strokes Auaha domain skill, used when a workflow
falls inside the creative industries but no narrower skill exists yet.
Typical contexts:

- Drafting creative briefs, scopes of work, and statements of work
  for design, film, photography, publishing, or music projects.
- Drafting creative-services contracts, retainer agreements, and IP
  assignment / licence terms.
- Reviewing advertising claims for Fair Trading Act 1986 s.9
  (misleading and deceptive conduct) risk.
- Reviewing creative work for Advertising Standards Authority (ASA)
  code compliance.
- Copyright and moral rights questions under the Copyright Act 1994.

## What this skill will NOT do

- Provide legal advice. Outputs are working drafts only — they go to
  the creative business and, where appropriate, an IP or commercial
  lawyer.
- Sign or commit a creative-services contract on behalf of either
  party.
- Approve a campaign for release. Sign-off is the client's call,
  with input from the agency, the legal adviser, and (where the
  campaign claim is strong) the ASA self-regulatory process.
- Lodge ASA complaints or responses on behalf of an advertiser.
- Make a binding determination on whether content infringes
  copyright. The Copyright Tribunal and the courts decide; the
  skill flags risk.
- File a copyright assignment — assignments must be in writing and
  signed by the assignor, but the document execution is the parties',
  not the skill's.

## Tikanga check

Creative industries sit inside the Auaha kete and frequently touch
mātauranga Māori, Māori imagery, te reo Māori, and tribal taonga.

- Defer to the assembl-core `tikanga-compliance` skill on every
  customer-facing creative deliverable. Macrons, banned-words list,
  and reserved taonga terms are non-negotiable.
- Do not generate kowhaiwhai, tā moko, or whakairo imagery.
- Do not invent te reo phrases for branding. Where te reo appears
  in creative work, defer to a kaitiaki or Te Hiku Media.
- Where the creative work depicts a specific iwi, hapū, marae, or
  tribal taonga, consult mana whenua before publication.

## Privacy Act check

Creative work involves personal information at several points:
client and crew lists (IPP 5 storage and security), model and
talent releases (IPP 3 notice; IPP 11 disclosure when the asset
goes to clients), and audience targeting in advertising
(IPP 1 minimisation, IPP 11 cross-border transfers when ad
platforms are offshore — IPP 12).

Treat model and talent releases as the lawful basis for using a
person's image; without a release, do not include identifiable
people in commercial work.

## Workflow steps

For any Auaha workflow:

1. Identify the deliverable (brief, scope, contract, campaign asset,
   invoice).
2. Apply the assembl-core mandatory skills first
   (`tikanga-compliance` on customer-facing copy,
   `nz-privacy-act-2020` on any personal information).
3. Apply the relevant statute lens — Copyright Act 1994 for IP,
   Fair Trading Act 1986 for advertising claims, Contract and
   Commercial Law Act 2017 for contract drafting.
4. Stage the draft for the creative business and, where the work
   carries legal risk (campaign claims, IP assignment, sponsorship
   disclosure), for legal review.

assigned.
