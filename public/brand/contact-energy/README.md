## Tone of voice — observed

Plain, benefit-first, everyday NZ English. Short imperative verbs ("Pay bill", "Find out more"). Offers lead with the number ("Get up to $50 off your power bill every month for a year"). T&Cs footnoted honestly and immediately. No jargon, no hype adjectives. Warm but functional — closer to a utility you trust than a lifestyle brand. Consumer-care language is prominent ("how we'll keep you safe and connected").

Write demo copy the way they do: benefit + number + plain verb. Sentence case everywhere.

## Demo implications

- Contact red `#E62A32`/`#D91C24` is THE primary on every customer-facing surface; Montserrat 900 headings
- Paper `#F9F8F8` background, `#303030` ink, pill CTAs
- assembl/Assembling elements (credit tally, wait-state earn cards, matariki dot loader) carry champagne gold `#BFA37A` — never red — so the earn layer reads as a distinct, bolted-on stratum (Tier 2 slice positioning)
- Greeting "kia ora {name}" is consistent with mainstream NZ utility tone

## Hard rules

- **NEVER claim active partnership** — all copy frames "what a Contact × Assembling partnership could look like"
- **NEVER mint real bill credits** — label everything `demo · illustrative figures · not real credits`
- **NEVER fabricate customer data** — usage charts labelled `(demo)`, fictional persona only, no real-looking ICP numbers/meter numbers/addresses/phone numbers
- lowercase `assembl`; Kate Hudson (not Harland)
- English-led; te reo only where it earns its place (kia ora greeting, matariki loader naming)
- Economics figures must cite Contact's public annual reporting
